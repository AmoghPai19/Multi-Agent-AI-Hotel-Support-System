# Pet & Service Animal Policy

**Policy ID:** POL-PET-011 | **Effective Date:** 2026-01-01 | **Version:** 1.3
**Owner:** Guest Relations

## 1. Service Animals (Not "Pets")
- Service animals as defined by applicable disability law (e.g., ADA in the US: dogs, and in limited cases miniature horses, individually trained to perform a task for a person with a disability) are welcome at **no charge and no restriction on room type**, and are never treated as pets.
- Staff may only ask two questions if it is not obvious the animal is a service animal: (1) is the animal required because of a disability, and (2) what task has it been trained to perform. Staff must **never** ask for documentation, certification, or proof of the guest's disability — none is required or legally sufficient to demand under ADA-equivalent law.
- Emotional support animals, comfort animals, and therapy animals **do not** have the same legal status as service animals in most jurisdictions (post-2021 US DOT/ADA guidance treats them as pets for lodging purposes) and are handled under the standard pet policy below, unless local law states otherwise.

## 2. Standard Pet Policy
- Pet-friendly designated rooms/floors only; pets are not permitted in non-designated rooms, food & beverage outlets (beyond legally required exceptions), or pool areas.
- Typical limits: up to 2 pets per room, weight limit (commonly 50-75 lbs combined), dogs and cats only unless a property explicitly allows more.
- A non-refundable pet fee (commonly $50-$150 per stay, sometimes per night) applies, plus liability for any pet-caused damage per POL-DMG-010.
- Pets may not be left unattended in the room unless crated and for a limited duration; noise complaints from an unattended pet may result in a request to remove the pet from the property.

## 3. Compliance Notes for Automated Agents
- Automated agents must never ask a guest for medical documentation or "proof" for a claimed service animal.
- Automated agents must correctly distinguish a stated "service animal" (no fee, no restriction) from a stated "emotional support animal" or generic "pet" (standard pet policy applies) based on what the guest states, without probing into their disability.
- If a guest states their animal is a service animal, the agent may ask only the two permitted questions in §1 before proceeding — never more.
