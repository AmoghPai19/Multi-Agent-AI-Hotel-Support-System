# Force Majeure Policy

**Policy ID:** POL-FM-017 | **Effective Date:** 2026-01-01 | **Version:** 1.1
**Owner:** Legal / Revenue Management

## 1. Definition
Force majeure refers to extraordinary events beyond either party's reasonable control that prevent normal performance of a reservation or contract, including: natural disasters (hurricanes, earthquakes, floods, wildfires), government-declared states of emergency or travel bans, mandatory evacuation orders, war/terrorism, widespread infrastructure failure (e.g., prolonged power/water outage at the property), and public health emergencies with an official government advisory against travel.

Force majeure does **not** include: ordinary bad weather that does not trigger an official advisory/closure, a guest's personal illness (handled under standard cancellation exception review, POL-CXL-002 §4), or general travel inconvenience (traffic, routine flight delays without broader disruption).

## 2. Guest Remedies When Force Majeure Applies
- Full cancellation penalty waiver for reservations directly affected by a qualifying event in the guest's origin or the property's location within the relevant travel window.
- Rebooking flexibility (date change without penalty) is offered as the default remedy where the guest wishes to still stay; a refund is offered if the guest cannot or does not wish to rebook.
- Non-refundable/prepaid rates are also waived under confirmed force majeure — this is one of the few situations where a "non-refundable" rate is refunded in full.

## 3. Property-Side Force Majeure (Property Cannot Operate)
If the property itself cannot operate (e.g., mandatory evacuation, structural damage, extended utility failure) during a guest's confirmed dates, the property must offer a full refund or, where feasible, relocation assistance similar in spirit to the Walked-Guest remedies (POL-OVBK-008), since the guest is not at fault.

## 4. Determination Authority
Only senior management/legal (not automated agents or front-line staff) may formally declare that a given event qualifies as force majeure for policy purposes, based on official government/weather-authority declarations. Until such a determination is made, standard cancellation policy applies, with reasonable customer-service discretion for imminent, obviously qualifying events (e.g., an active hurricane warning for the property's area).

## 5. Compliance Notes for Automated Agents
- Automated agents may reference an active, officially declared emergency (e.g., a published NOAA hurricane warning or government travel advisory) as sufficient grounds to apply the force-majeure waiver without further escalation, but should log the specific advisory referenced.
- For ambiguous or not-yet-officially-declared situations, the agent should escalate to a human for a case-by-case, discretionary judgment call rather than deny or approve the waiver outright.
